System foundations

Sources & Stakeholders

Understand where the heat signal comes from, what happens to it before a warning is issued, and who uses each layer of information.

Primary data sources

01 · AWS

Automatic Weather Stations

Ground stations provide direct observations such as temperature and humidity. In a production pipeline, these readings would be checked for freshness and quality before analysis.

02 · IMD

India Meteorological Department

Historical climatology, meteorological observations and official warnings provide institutional context for understanding whether conditions are abnormal and how alerts should be communicated.

03 · SATELLITE

Thermal satellite feeds

Land-surface temperature and broader spatial patterns can complement point observations, helping the hotspot layer represent heat concentration beyond individual stations.

Forecast → alert workflow

01 · ACQUIRE

Collect

Bring together field and remote-sensing observations.

02 · VALIDATE

Check quality

Identify missing, inconsistent or suspicious observations.

03 · PREDICT

Forecast heat

Estimate future maximum temperature and persistence by region.

04 · CLASSIFY

Assign level

Map the resulting heat signal to a consistent severity language.

05 · DISSEMINATE

Communicate

Publish a bulletin, dashboard indicator or advisory for the right audience.

Stakeholders & their needs

METEOROLOGISTS

Analytical review

  • Inspect observations and anomalies.
  • Review forecast behaviour and persistence.
  • Validate warning context before dissemination.
DISASTER MANAGEMENT

Operational response

  • Identify affected areas and severity.
  • Coordinate field teams and response measures.
  • Distribute approved warnings and advisories.
GENERAL PUBLIC

Simple action

  • See what the current warning means.
  • Read practical heat-health precautions.
  • Subscribe for demonstration alerts.

Data integrity principles

  • Sample values on this website are for demonstration.
  • Production alerts should never be generated from unchecked observations.
  • Forecasts should be revised as new evidence arrives.
  • Official bulletins remain the authoritative source for public safety decisions.
